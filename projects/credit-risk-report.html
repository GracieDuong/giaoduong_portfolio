Section 02 · Risk Segmentation

Risk is unevenly distributed —
concentrated across 4 key dimensions

Breaking down bad loan rate by credit grade, term, DTI, and interest rate reveals clear risk concentrations. Grade G and 60-month loans are the two largest red flags.

Bad Loan Rate by Credit Grade
Clear gradient from A (safe) to G (very high risk)
Grade A: 5.7%, Grade G: 31.3%.
Loan Volume by Credit Grade
Most of the portfolio is Grade A-B — but C-G requires close monitoring
Grade B has the most loans (11,674), Grade G the fewest (313).
Grades F & G exceed 30% bad loan rate: While Grade A sits at just 5.7%, Grade F reaches 30.25% and Grade G 31.31% — meaning 1 in every 3 Grade G loans is charged off. Although F+G represent only 3.5% of the portfolio by count, this is the highest-concentration risk zone.

The data story in summary: The 2021 portfolio has solid foundations — steady growth, a 1.086x recovery ratio, and 86.18% good loans. The challenge is extreme risk polarization: Grade A sits at 5.7% bad rate while Grade G reaches 31.3%.
