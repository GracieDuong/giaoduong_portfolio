Data Assumptions & Limitations

The dataset contains historical consumer loan applications including borrower information, loan status, funded amount, total payments received, interest rate, DTI, credit grade, loan term, and purpose.

The dataset does not include repayment schedules, Days Past Due (DPD), collection actions, or monthly loan performance snapshots. True DPD monitoring and vintage curve analysis cannot be performed directly.

This report uses the following as proxy indicators for portfolio risk:

  • Loan status (Fully Paid / Current / Charged Off)
  • Bad loan rate by origination month as a risk trend proxy
  • Repayment indicator (received / funded) as a portfolio health signal
  • Grade, term, DTI, and purpose segmentation for concentration analysis

Section 04 · Recommendations

Four priority actions for risk control

Based on bad loan rate concentration and capital at risk, four actions are identified — from tighter origination controls to automated monitoring triggers.

● High Priority
60-month loans: 22.34% bad loan rate
Long-term exposure with $163M in funded amount and double the default risk of 36-month loans.
Action: Apply stricter origination criteria for 60-month loans. Monitor this segment separately from the overall portfolio with its own risk dashboard.
Monitor: Bad rate by term (monthly)
● High Priority
Grades F & G exceed 30% bad loan rate
1 in 3 Grade G loans defaults. Highest credit risk concentration in the portfolio.
Action: Limit new exposure to Grades F–G. Introduce tiered approval criteria and consider collateral requirements for Grade E and below.
Monitor: Bad rate by grade (quarterly)
● Medium Priority
Small business loans: 25.62% bad loan rate
Highest bad loan rate by purpose. Segment-level concentration driven by SME business cycle volatility.
Action: Build a separate SME credit review pipeline. Add revenue verification and industry-specific underwriting rules.
Monitor: Bad rate by purpose (monthly)
● Medium Priority
High DTI borrowers show elevated risk
High DTI band at 15.6% — 30% above the Low DTI band — indicating weaker repayment capacity under financial stress.
Action: Introduce DTI trigger rules in the scoring system. Automate early-warning alerts when DTI exceeds 20%.
Monitor: Bad rate by DTI band (monthly)

The core finding: The 2021 portfolio is fundamentally healthy — steady growth, 1.086x repayment indicator, and 86.18% good loan rate. The challenge is that risk is not uniformly distributed. The same portfolio that produces 5.7% bad loan rate in Grade A produces 31.3% in Grade G. The business response is not to slow down origination, but to apply more granular risk controls by grade, term, purpose, and DTI — so that growth and quality move together.
